# CCA-F Exam Preparation

## Lesson 7: Project Planning and Delegation

**Course:** AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations
**Lesson:** 7 of 14
**Duration:** ~3 minutes

---

# 1. What is this lesson about?

This lesson is a **practical application of Delegation**.

Instead of learning another theoretical concept, you select a real, medium-sized project and work with Claude to:

1. Define the project's vision and goals.
2. Break the project into major tasks.
3. Analyze which tasks should involve humans, AI, or both.
4. Create a project plan.
5. Save the plan for use in later lessons.

The important idea is:

> **Delegation should be applied to an entire project, not just to isolated prompts or individual tasks.**

---

# 2. Step 1 — Choose Your Project

The first step is to select a **medium-sized, multi-step project**.

The project should satisfy three conditions:

### 1. Substantial

It should contain multiple types of tasks.

### 2. Manageable

It should be possible to complete in approximately **one hour of work**.

### 3. Meaningful

It should be something you are genuinely interested in creating or accomplishing.

---

# 3. Types of Projects

The lesson provides several categories.

## Communication Projects

Examples:

* Create a presentation.
* Write a series of posts explaining a complex topic.
* Develop a proposal or pitch.
* Create a professional or personal bio.

---

## Research Projects

Examples:

* Research an emerging technology or trend.
* Analyze a dataset and identify patterns.
* Compare products, services, or approaches.
* Investigate a historical event.

---

## Creative Projects

Examples:

* Outline a short story.
* Design a simple website structure.
* Develop a product or service concept.

---

## Learning Projects

Examples:

* Create a learning plan.
* Build a collection of resources.
* Develop a tutorial.
* Create study materials.

The key is not the particular project type.

The key is that the project should contain **multiple tasks that can be analyzed for delegation**.

---

# 4. Step 2 — Define the Project Vision

Once you've selected the project, start a conversation with Claude.

Don't simply tell Claude:

> "Here is my project. Make a plan."

Instead, have a conversation.

Ask Claude questions and allow Claude to ask you questions.

The goal is to establish a clear vision for the final result.

---

# 5. What Should the Project Vision Define?

You should reach clarity about two important things:

### What does success look like?

Define the desired outcome.

For example:

> "A presentation that allows senior engineers to understand the proposed architecture and make a decision."

### What would make the project valuable?

The project should have a meaningful purpose.

For example:

> "The presentation should help the team understand why the proposed architecture is better than the current architecture."

The official lesson specifically asks you to work with Claude until you have a clear picture of **what success looks like** and **what makes the project valuable or meaningful**.

---

# 6. Why Use a Conversation?

This is an important detail.

The lesson does **not** simply ask you to exchange statements with Claude.

It recommends a genuine conversation.

That means:

* Claude asks questions.
* You clarify requirements.
* You challenge assumptions.
* Claude proposes ideas.
* You ask follow-up questions.
* You refine your vision.

### The objective

Discover information that may not have been obvious when you first started.

---

# 7. Step 3 — Break the Project Into Tasks

After defining the vision, identify the **major tasks** needed to complete the project.

For example, suppose your project is:

> Create an architecture presentation.

You might identify:

```text
Project
   ↓
Research current architecture
   ↓
Identify problems
   ↓
Research alternatives
   ↓
Compare alternatives
   ↓
Select recommendation
   ↓
Create architecture diagrams
   ↓
Create presentation
   ↓
Review and refine
```

This is the **task breakdown**.

---

# 8. Analyze Each Task Through Delegation

For every major task, discuss five questions.

## Question 1 — What skills, knowledge, or AI capabilities are needed?

Ask:

> What does this task require?

Examples:

* Domain expertise
* Research
* Writing
* Data analysis
* Programming
* Visual design
* Reasoning

---

## Question 2 — What benefits from uniquely human strengths?

Ask:

> Where is human expertise particularly valuable?

Examples:

* Business judgment
* Domain knowledge
* Creativity
* Ethical decisions
* Understanding organizational context
* Final decision-making

---

## Question 3 — What can leverage AI capabilities?

Ask:

> Which parts are particularly suitable for AI?

Examples:

* Drafting
* Summarization
* Brainstorming
* Generating alternatives
* Pattern analysis
* Code generation
* Repetitive work

---

## Question 4 — Where can collaboration have the most impact?

Some tasks are neither purely human nor purely AI.

They may benefit most from:

> **Human + AI collaboration.**

For example:

```text
Human
  ↓
Provides architecture context
  ↓
Claude
  ↓
Generates alternatives
  ↓
Human + Claude
  ↓
Discuss trade-offs
  ↓
Human
  ↓
Makes final decision
```

The lesson specifically asks you to identify where collaboration could have the greatest impact.

---

# 9. Challenge Assumptions

This is an important part of the exercise.

Don't simply accept your initial delegation plan.

During your conversation with Claude:

* Challenge assumptions.
* Ask for clarification.
* Consider alternatives.
* Stay open to unexpected insights.

### Why?

Because the purpose of the conversation is not just to produce a task list.

The interaction itself may reveal:

* Tasks you forgot
* Better ways to divide work
* Risks
* Opportunities for collaboration
* Tasks that should remain human-controlled

---

# 10. Create the Project Plan

After analyzing the tasks, create a project plan containing:

* Major tasks
* Delegation decisions

A simple structure could be:

| Task                  | Human | AI | Collaboration | Reason                      |
| --------------------- | ----- | -- | ------------- | --------------------------- |
| Define objectives     | ✓     |    | ✓             | Business context            |
| Research options      |       | ✓  | ✓             | AI can analyze alternatives |
| Architecture decision | ✓     |    | ✓             | Requires expert judgment    |
| Draft presentation    |       | ✓  | ✓             | AI is good at drafting      |
| Final review          | ✓     |    |               | Human accountability        |

The important part is not the exact table format.

The important part is that **each major task has a deliberate delegation decision**.

# 14. Delegation Analysis

### Task: Analyze existing code

**AI:** Useful

AI can:

* Identify patterns.
* Find deprecated APIs.
* Summarize modules.
* Identify dependencies.

**Human:** Required

The architect understands the business and operational context.

**Decision:** Collaboration.

---

### Task: Research modernization options

**AI:** Strong candidate.

Claude can compare:

* Approaches
* Trade-offs
* Migration strategies

**Decision:** AI + human review.

---

### Task: Select final architecture

**AI:** Can provide alternatives.

**Human:** Makes the final decision.

Why?

Because the decision involves:

* Business constraints
* Security
* Cost
* Team expertise
* Operational requirements

**Decision:** Human-led collaboration.

---

### Task: Generate migration checklist

**AI:** Strong candidate.

Claude can create a first draft.

**Decision:** AI with human review.

---

# 15. The Important Delegation Principle

The lesson is teaching you to think in terms of:

> **Task → Required capability → Human strengths → AI strengths → Collaboration opportunity → Delegation decision**

Not:

> **Task → Ask Claude to do everything**

# 18. Important CCA-F Exam Point

**Delegation is not a one-time decision.**

For a multi-step project, different tasks may require different delegation approaches.

For example:

```text
Task A → Human
Task B → AI
Task C → Human + AI
Task D → AI
Task E → Human
```

Therefore, you should evaluate delegation **at the task level**.

---

# 19. Another Important Exam Point

A project does not have to be completely human-driven or completely AI-driven.

There are three possibilities for individual tasks:

```text
Human
   OR
AI
   OR
Human + AI
```

The correct choice depends on:

* Task requirements
* Human strengths
* AI capabilities
* Risk
* Desired outcome

# 22. Common Exam Traps

## Trap 1

> "Choose a very large project so you can demonstrate more AI capabilities."

**Incorrect.**

The project should be substantial but manageable within approximately one hour.

---

## Trap 2

> "Define the task list first and then decide what success means."

**Not the recommended approach.**

First establish a clear vision and understand what success looks like.

---

## Trap 3

> "Claude should automatically determine the delegation plan."

**Incorrect.**

The delegation plan is developed through a conversation where human and AI explore the tasks and challenge assumptions.

---

## Trap 4

> "Every task should either be assigned to humans or AI."

**Incorrect.**

Some tasks benefit most from **human-AI collaboration**.

---

## Trap 5

> "Once the delegation plan is created, it is no longer useful."

**Incorrect.**

The project plan is saved because it will be used later to practice Description, Discernment, and Diligence.

# 29. Final CCA-F Takeaway

The most important concept from this lesson is:

> **Delegation should be applied deliberately at the project and task level.**

A good delegation plan starts by understanding the project:

**What are we trying to accomplish?**

Then breaks it into tasks:

**What work needs to be done?**

Then evaluates each task:

**What requires human strengths?**

**What can AI do well?**

**Where can human-AI collaboration have the most impact?**

Finally:

> **Create and save a project plan containing the major tasks and delegation decisions.**

This plan becomes the foundation for practicing the remaining AI Fluency competencies: **Description, Discernment, and Diligence**.
